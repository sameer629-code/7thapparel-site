CONTACT & STORY

We built this because we watched it go wrong.

Our story

For years we did what most good factories do: we made clothes for other people's brands and shipped them out of the country. Denim and jersey for the UK. Fleece programmes for the United States. Twill workwear for Italy. The garments were right, the dates were met, and the customers came back.

But we kept watching the same thing happen to the people who wanted to buy from us and could not.

A brand in Amsterdam or Berlin or Copenhagen would find us, love the price, and then hit the wall that has nothing to do with clothing. Their bank wanted a letter of credit that tied up cash for months. Nobody wanted to be the importer of record for a container from Asia. They needed a customs broker they did not have. They were being asked to wire a five-figure deposit to a company they could not look up, in a country they had never visited, with no idea what they would do if the goods were wrong.

And underneath all of it, the quiet one: they could not order small. Every factory wanted a thousand pieces per colour. A brand with a good idea and no warehouse had to bet the whole season on a guess, or give up.

So the answer was not a better factory. We already had the factory. The answer was a European company standing in front of it — one that carries the import, the customs, the currency and the risk, so that buying from us costs a brand no more effort than buying from a supplier down the road.

That is what 7th Apparel is. Same owners, same floor, same people who have been cutting and sewing since 2011 — with the paperwork moved to our side of the table.

What we are actually trying to do

  • Make small orders normal. Ten pieces should not be an insult to a factory. It should be how a brand finds out whether the idea works before spending real money.
  • Take the risk off the buyer. No letter of credit, no importing, no customs broker, no wiring money into the unknown. One European invoice, in euros.
  • Say what is true. Real prices published on the site. Real order history with real customer names. If we cannot do something, we say so before you have paid for it.
  • Let people watch. You should be able to see your order move through the floor rather than wait six weeks in silence and hope.
