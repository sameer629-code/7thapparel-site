FAQ

The questions buyers actually ask

Including the awkward ones. If your question is not here, send it — we will answer it properly and then add it to this page.

Why not Portugal or Turkey?

Cost, mostly, and it is a big gap — you will typically pay 35–55% less per piece than Portugal for the same construction, and our GSP+ origin means the duty into the EU is nil on most cotton garments where Turkey and Portugal carry none either but sit at a much higher base price. What you give up is proximity: a physical sample takes days longer to reach you, and a factory visit is a longer flight. If you need two-week turnarounds on repeats, Portugal wins. If you need a real price on a real quantity with a European company standing behind it, this is the better trade.

You own a clothing brand yourself. How do I know you will not copy my design?

Fair question, and we would rather answer it than have you wonder. The group does own 7thstreet, a consumer brand — it sells in its home market only, and it has never carried a design made for a client. Our standard terms assign your designs, patterns, tech packs and blocks to you, and bar us from producing them for anyone, ourselves included. We will sign your NDA before you send anything, or send you ours. If that is not enough, ask us for a reference from a buyer whose designs we have held for two years.

What happens if you are late?

So far it has not happened — thirteen orders, none late. That is a small sample and we will not pretend otherwise. What we commit to is this: your delivery date is confirmed in writing before you pay a deposit, you see the order move through the tracking system so a slip is visible weeks before it becomes a problem, and if we are the reason a date moves, we cover the air-freight difference to get you back on schedule. That last one is in the quote, not just on this page.

How do you get fabric for a 10-piece order when mills sell in 300-metre minimums?

Three ways. We hold stock qualities in the fabrics we run most — jersey, fleece, piqué, 12 oz denim, 3×3 twill — so a capsule can come off fabric already on our shelf. We can piggyback your small run on a larger cut of the same quality when the timing lines up. And for anything we have to buy in specially, we will tell you the minimum before you commit rather than after. It is the honest reason a ten-piece run costs more per piece: you are carrying the set-up, not the fabric efficiency.

Do you already make for a brand like mine?

Probably, and we will tell you plainly if there is a direct conflict. We currently ship denim and jersey for a UK brand, fleece programmes for a US buyer, twill workwear for an Italian label, denim jackets and cotton trousers for two more US accounts. We do not run exclusivity by category — there is not a factory in the world that could — but we do not share your tech packs, your fabric sources or your costings with anyone, and we will not put your pattern on someone else's order.

What about political unrest, port delays or an Eid shutdown?

Our floor is inland and our fabric comes from domestic mills, so the disruptions that hit us are labour holidays rather than shipping lanes. Eid closes the floor twice a year for roughly a week each time, and those dates are published years ahead — we build them into your delivery date rather than discovering them later. Karachi port congestion is the real variable and it moves sailing dates by days, not months; we quote the sailing, not the arrival, and tell you which is which.

Can I visit the factory?

Yes, and you should if the order is big enough to justify the flight. We will host you, walk the floor and show you whatever you want to see. If you cannot travel, we do the reverse: a live video walk-through on your schedule, plus a physical sample box of garments off our own line so you can judge the quality with your hands rather than through a screen.

What are your actual defect rates?

We inspect to AQL 2.5 on the major, which means an accepted lot has under 2.5% major defects by the standard sampling plan. Across the thirteen orders we have shipped no lot has been rejected on arrival and no customer has raised a quality claim. We would rather show you than tell you — ask for the inspection report from any order we have run and we will send it, including the minor non-conformances and how they were closed.

Is that everything you can make?

No — what is on the site is a sample of what we are asked for most, not the limit. The floor is a one-stop operation: fabric sourcing, design and pattern, cutting, sewing, printing, embroidery, labelling, packing and logistics, all under one roof. Between knit, woven, denim, workwear and leather we cover most of what a clothing brand or a company buying uniform actually needs — jeans in four- and five-pocket, chinos, cargos, shorts, towels in cotton terry, joggers, dungarees, coveralls, scrubs, rain suits, hi-vis, leather in cowhide, sheepskin, goat and buffalo. If the garment you want is not shown anywhere on this site, send us a photo or a sketch. The answer is usually yes, and when it is no we will tell you straight rather than take the order and struggle.

Do you do organic cotton?

Yes — 100% organic cotton across knitwear and woven, through the same mills we use for conventional qualities. It costs more per kilo and behaves differently in the wash, so we always sample it before you commit. Certificates are shared on request. We buy fabric per order and can source from more than one certified mill, so the paperwork is issued against your specific order rather than published as a generic certificate.

Why is the sample €50 when other factories send them free?

Because a free sample is never free — it is priced into the bulk, and it tells you nothing about whether the factory takes you seriously. A sample costs us several hundred euros in pattern-making, a designer's time and a sample tailor on a machine that has stopped running bulk. We charge €50, we credit it in full against your first order, and in return you get a real garment in the fabric you chose, with your label, rather than a printed blank.

What are the payment terms?

50% to start development and buy fabric, 40% before shipping, 10% after you have inspected the delivery. No letter of credit, no bank guarantee, no cash against documents — you are paying a European company on an ordinary invoice. Companies and clubs buying kit can usually get 30-day invoice terms up to €5,000 after a credit check.

I am not ready to order. What is the smallest first step?

Send us a brief with whatever you have — a sketch, a photo of a garment you like, a rough quantity. You get a reply the same working day and an itemised quote inside 48 hours, and it costs you nothing. If the numbers work, the next step is a €50 sample. Nobody will chase you between those two things.
